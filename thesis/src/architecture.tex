\chapter{System Architecture}
\label{chap:architecture}

This chapter specifies the design of the LLM-based repair layer that
this thesis adds to the FAIR Jupyter reproducibility
pipeline~\cite{samuel2024gigascience}. The existing pipeline ends at
detecting and logging notebook execution failures. Even its
containerised evolution~\cite{samuel2025docker} stops at the point of
execution: it records the residual failure, but does not explain or
repair it. The components described here attach at that point. They
carry a dependency-related failure through explanation, repair,
re-execution, and structured logging.

Following the taxonomy of Yang et al.~\cite{yang2025aprsurvey}, the
repair layer is built as a \emph{procedural pipeline}, not an agentic
framework: a fixed sequence of components, driven by a deterministic
orchestrator. This choice is deliberate. Agentic loops achieve strong
results on complex, multi-file bugs, but their latency and control
overhead scale poorly across a batch of dependency-error notebooks. The
interactive notebook-repair agents of Grotov et
al.~\cite{grotov2024untangling,grotov2024debug} also assume a live
kernel and a user in the loop -- neither is available in autonomous
batch processing.

Five non-functional requirements shape the design, and are referred to by
these labels throughout the thesis. Components rely only on open, locally
deployable models (NFR1). Explanations are written in plain language for a
non-expert reader (NFR2). Everything is documented and version-controlled
to support traceability and reproducibility of a run (NFR3). The layer
processes its full target set
without manual intervention (NFR4). And components are modular, with an
injected configuration surface rather than hard-coded settings (NFR5). One 
functional requirement is also referred to by a label: FR1. It determines 
whether a detected failure is a dependency error that falls within the system's 
pip-based repair scope.

The scope of this chapter is the \emph{contracts} between components and
the \emph{data} they exchange. Prompt design, model selection, and the
internals of the PyPI-grounded retrieval step belong to
\Cref{sec:m-explanation,sec:m-repair}. How each component was actually
built is the subject of \Cref{chap:methodology} as a whole.

\section{Design Overview}
\label{sec:design-overview}

The system can be stated in one sentence: \emph{when a notebook has
failed because of a dependency error, read that failure, explain it in
plain language, derive a fix from live PyPI data, apply the fix and
re-run the notebook to check it, and record the result.} That sentence
is partitioned into the five components of
\Cref{tab:components-objectives}, each realising one thesis objective,
sequenced by an orchestrator that realises pipeline integration (O5).

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{|l|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{2.8cm}|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{6.0cm}|}
    \hline
    \textbf{Component} & \textbf{Requirement}\newline\textbf{or objective} & \textbf{Responsibility} \\
    \hline
    ErrorClassifier  & FR1     & Classify the dependency failure, determine repair eligibility, and identify the failing module; also reclassify an error newly exposed by re-execution. \\
    \hline
    LLMExplainer     & O1      & Produce a plain-language explanation of each classified error, in Round 1 and for a newly exposed Round-2 error. \\
    \hline
    RAGRepairAgent   & O2      & Derive a concrete fix, grounded in live PyPI metadata, for a repair-eligible record. \\
    \hline
    FixApplicator    & O3      & Apply the fix and re-execute the notebook to validate it. \\
    \hline
    ResultLogger     & O4, O6  & Store repair attempts in SQLite and export them for knowledge-graph enrichment. \\
    \hline
    Orchestrator     & O5      & Sequence the five components; drive the bounded second round; expose the round and configuration switches. \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{The five repair components and the orchestrator, each mapped
           to the requirement or objective it realises.}

\end{table}

\section{Integration Point}
\label{sec:integration-point}

\begin{sloppypar}
The containerised FAIR Jupyter pipeline~\cite{samuel2025docker}
executes the notebooks of each repository inside a per-repository
Docker container. It writes one row per execution to the
\texttt{notebook\_executions} table, and tags each failure with an
\texttt{error\_category}. A pre-existing categorisation routine
(\texttt{summary.py}) already maps \texttt{ModuleNotFoundError} and
\texttt{ImportError} to \texttt{DEPENDENCY\_ERROR}. The repair layer
activates on exactly those rows: it performs a batch pass over the
\texttt{notebook\_executions} rows whose \texttt{error\_category =
DEPENDENCY\_ERROR}.
\end{sloppypar}

\paragraph{Re-execution substrate.}
A candidate fix is validated by re-running the notebook from beginning to
end inside a newly built Docker environment. The environment uses the
same Python base image and installation procedure as the upstream Docker
pipeline (\Cref{sec:m-fixapply-docker}). This reduces differences between
the original and repaired executions. However, it does not reproduce the
original environment exactly. The repository is cloned again when the
repair is tested, and the final evaluation used its current default
branch rather than the exact revision that originally failed
(\Cref{sec:v-final-manifest}).

\begin{sloppypar}
\paragraph{Context available at the hook-in point.}
Three kinds of context are distinguished. Directly available, from the
Docker pipeline's result row, are
\texttt{error\_type}, \texttt{error\_message}, \texttt{error\_cell\_index},
\texttt{notebook\_id}, \texttt{repository\_id}, \texttt{notebook\_name},
\texttt{url}, \texttt{total\_code\_cells}, \texttt{executed\_cells}
(and \texttt{error\_category}); these form the raw input to the repair
layer. Additional context can be recovered when needed. A legacy execution table
may provide a traceback, and the notebook source can be fetched again from
GitHub (\Cref{sec:m-classification}). These sources are treated with lower
confidence because they may not correspond exactly to the original failed
execution. The original repository clone and container were not
preserved. Therefore, when a repair is tested, FixApplicator clones the
repository again and rebuilds the Docker environment
(\Cref{sec:m-fixapply-docker}).
\end{sloppypar}

\paragraph{Working set.}
The rows the repair layer activates on are the 214
\texttt{DEPENDENCY\_ERROR} records of \Cref{chap:problem-dataset}, of which
200 fall inside the pip-only repair scope. Those counts, the subtypes
behind them, and the development/evaluation split of the 200 are all fixed
there and are not restated in this chapter.

\Cref{tab:components-objectives} already ties to a requirement or

\subsection{ErrorClassifier}
\label{subsec:errorclassifier}
\begin{sloppypar}
ErrorClassifier decides whether a failure is an in-scope dependency error
eligible for pip-based repair and, if so, extracts the failing module and
its subtype.
It reuses the pipeline's existing \texttt{error\_category} tag as a first
filter and refines the decision from \texttt{error\_type} and
\texttt{error\_message}. The same rules are applied a second time when
re-execution exposes a new error.\\
\textbf{Input.} A \texttt{notebook\_executions} row (the fields listed in
Section~\ref{sec:integration-point}), or, in the second round, the new
error type and message that FixApplicator observed after re-execution.\\
\textbf{Output.} A classification carrying three values:
\texttt{failing\_module}, \texttt{subtype}, and \texttt{scope\_status}.
For an original \texttt{DEPENDENCY\_ERROR} record the subtype is one of
the four dependency-related values \texttt{missing\_package},
\texttt{wrong\_version}, \texttt{system\_library}, or
\texttt{mapping\_unknown}, and the scope status is \texttt{usable} or
\texttt{excluded}. A newly exposed error is reclassified with the same
rules; if it is not a dependency error at all (in the final evaluation, an
\texttt{IndexError}), its subtype is \texttt{out\_of\_scope} and it is
\texttt{excluded} from repair. It is still explained.
\end{sloppypar}

\textbf{Explanation is not gated by scope.} \texttt{scope\_status} decides
whether RAGRepairAgent and FixApplicator run on a row, not whether
LLMExplainer does. Every classified row, including an \texttt{excluded}
one, is still passed to LLMExplainer, for the reason given in
\Cref{sec:pd-explanation-scope}: a researcher whose failure falls outside
the pip-only repair scope still deserves an explanation of what went wrong.

\subsection{LLMExplainer}
\label{subsec:llmexplainer}
LLMExplainer generates a plain-language explanation of the classified
error for a non-expert reader (NFR2).\\
\textbf{Input.} One classified record: the error context (error type,
message, failing module, relevant cell) of the original failure in Round 1,
or of the newly exposed and reclassified error in Round 2.\\
\textbf{Output.} A schema-validated explanation object and the identifier
of the model that produced it (\texttt{model\_used}), forwarded to the
ResultLogger for logging in the \texttt{explanation} and
\texttt{llm\_model} columns of \texttt{repair\_attempts}.\\
\textbf{Independence from repair.} The explanation is produced for the
reader, not for the pipeline. RAGRepairAgent never receives it, and a
failed explanation call does not stop an otherwise repair-eligible record
from being repaired. This holds in both rounds.\\
\emph{The prompt template and model are defined in
\Cref{chap:methodology}; this component's contract is fixed here, its
prompt internals are not.}

\subsection{RAGRepairAgent}
\label{subsec:repairagent}
RAGRepairAgent derives a concrete repair for a classified, repair-eligible
error. Following PLLM~\cite{bartlett2025pllm}, the proposal is grounded in
live PyPI data rather than relying only on the model's training
knowledge.\\
\textbf{Input.} The classified error record, including the error type and
message, failing module, subtype, and root-cause hint, together with the
PyPI evidence retrieved for the resolved package. In Round 2, the same
contract is applied to the newly exposed and reclassified error.\\
\textbf{Output.} A \emph{fix object} (Section~\ref{subsec:fix-object}), or
an abstention when the available evidence is insufficient to support a
repair.\\
\emph{The retrieval process, version selection, use of compatibility
evidence, and the decision not to use repository dependency declarations
as repair evidence are described in \Cref{sec:m-repair}.}

\subsection{FixApplicator}
\label{subsec:fixapplicator}
\begin{sloppypar}
FixApplicator applies the validated repair inside a rebuilt Docker
environment and re-executes the notebook to check whether the repair
worked. The notebook is always run from the beginning, because notebook
state can depend on earlier cells and a partial re-run would not provide a
reliable validation~\cite{grotov2024debug}.\\
\textbf{Input.} A fix object together with the notebook and repository
information needed to rebuild the execution environment. Repository
dependency files such as \texttt{requirements.txt} and \texttt{setup.py},
when available, are used during this reconstruction
(\Cref{sec:m-fixapply-docker}). In Round 2, the rebuilt environment first
reapplies the validated Round-1 fix before applying the Round-2 repair, so
both fixes are tested together (\Cref{sec:m-fixapply-round2}).\\
\textbf{Output.} An outcome
$\in \{\texttt{fixed},\ \texttt{still\_failing},\ \texttt{apply\_error}\}$,
together with the new error type and message when execution still fails,
and an indication of whether the new error matches the one targeted by the
repair.
\end{sloppypar}

\subsection{ResultLogger}
\label{subsec:resultlogger}
\begin{sloppypar}
ResultLogger persists structured repair-attempt data. It is the source of
the benchmark dataset (O4) and, through its CSV export, the input to the
knowledge-graph mapping (O6); it does not write RDF itself.\\
\textbf{Input.} The classification, explanation, fix object, outcome, and
run metadata of one repair-decision entry.\\
\textbf{Output.} One \texttt{repair\_attempts} row per repair-decision
entry (Section~\ref{subsec:repair-table}), whether the agent proposed a
fix or abstained. Normally such an entry is a RAGRepairAgent invocation;
an original out-of-scope record is represented by a synthesised abstention
entry without an agent or model call. A Round-1 row carries the
explanation of the original
failure; a Round-2 row carries the explanation of the newly exposed error
that round targeted. A CSV export of the table is the logical source of
the RML mapping (Section~\ref{subsec:kg}), which was pilot-validated and
then run, unchanged, over the complete table of the final evaluation
(\Cref{sec:v-i6-fullscale}).\\
\emph{How the four upstream outputs are joined into that single row is an
implementation concern rather than a contract one, and is described in
\Cref{sec:m-resultlogger}.}
\end{sloppypar}

\section{Orchestration and Control Flow}
\label{sec:orchestrator}

The orchestrator iterates over the target rows and runs the components in
order: classify, explain, check repair eligibility, repair, apply, log. A
repair-agent invocation does not always lead to a fix application: when
retrieval cannot ground a candidate, RAGRepairAgent abstains, nothing is
applied, and the abstention is logged as its own row. Three properties are
fixed at this level.

The orchestrator calls each of ErrorClassifier through ResultLogger
through that component's own interface, rather than reimplementing any of
them. It adds only two things of its own: the bounded second round
described below, and a single run identifier shared by every call it makes
for a given row.

\paragraph{The bounded second round.}
The default is a single repair round. In two-round mode, a second round
is considered when Round 1 reached FixApplicator, the notebook came back
\texttt{still\_failing}, and the error it now fails on is a genuinely new
one rather than the original error again. The new error is then treated as
a record of its own and walks the same path as an original failure.
ErrorClassifier reclassifies it. LLMExplainer explains it, so that the
reader receives an account of every error the pipeline encountered, not
only the first. Only then is repair eligibility evaluated: a newly exposed
\texttt{missing\_package} or \texttt{wrong\_version} error proceeds to
RAGRepairAgent and FixApplicator, while a newly exposed
\texttt{system\_library}, \texttt{mapping\_unknown}, or non-dependency error
is explained but not repaired. The explanation step is independent of the
repair step in both directions: RAGRepairAgent never sees the explanation,
and a failed explanation call does not prevent an eligible record from
being repaired. The repair process stops after the second round.
An error exposed by the Round-2 re-execution is recorded but is neither 
reclassified, explained, nor repaired.

This feedback structure follows the re-execution-based repair approach of
ChatRepair~\cite{xia2024chatrepair}, but uses a fixed two-round budget
instead of an open loop. The evaluation does not run separate one-round
and two-round experiments. Instead, both outcomes are taken from the same
two-round execution. This avoids introducing extra variation from the
language model's non-zero sampling temperature. The Round-1 result is
therefore kept unchanged, while Round 2 only extends it when a new eligible
error is exposed. \Cref{chap:validation} compares the Round-1 outcome with
the final outcome after up to two rounds.

\paragraph{Failure isolation (NFR4).}
Every attempt is logged, even when a component itself errors -- malformed
LLM output, an unreachable PyPI, or a re-execution timeout. Such cases are
caught and recorded with the appropriate \texttt{outcome}; a single
problematic notebook never aborts the batch.

\paragraph{Configuration surface (NFR5).}
The model, the prompt strategy, and the round count are injected per run
rather than hard-coded, so they can be swapped without changing component
code. The same injection points drive the model and prompt comparisons in
the evaluation.

\begin{verbatim}
for row in notebook_executions with DEPENDENCY_ERROR:
    rec  = ErrorClassifier(row)            # FR1: subtype, scope
    expl = LLMExplainer(rec)               # O1: every record
    if not rec.repair_eligible:         # explained, not repaired
        stub = build_excluded_repair_stub(rec)   # no agent call
        log(rec, expl, stub, None, round=1)
        continue
    fix = RAGRepairAgent(rec, PyPI)     # O2: proposal or abstain
    out = FixApplicator(fix, env) if fix else None  # O3
    log(rec, expl, fix, out, round=1)   # row even if abstained

    if two_round and out == 'still_failing' and out.new_error:
        rec2  = ErrorClassifier(out.new_error)   # reclassify
        expl2 = LLMExplainer(rec2)               # explain, always
        if not rec2.repair_eligible:
            continue                  # trace only, no Round 2
        fix2 = RAGRepairAgent(rec2, PyPI)
        out2 = FixApplicator(fix2, env + fix) if fix2 else None
        log(rec2, expl2, fix2, out2, round=2)  # cumulative env
    # hard cap: no third round
\end{verbatim}

A failure inside \texttt{LLMExplainer} is caught and recorded as a failed
explanation; it does not raise past the call, so the lines that follow it
run regardless.

\section{Data Contracts}
\label{sec:data-contracts}

\subsection{Fix Object}
\label{subsec:fix-object}

The RAGRepairAgent emits the \emph{proposed} fix as structured JSON,
before application. Version~1 is pip-only: \texttt{action} is one of
\texttt{install}, \texttt{pin\_version}, or \texttt{none}; \texttt{version}
is \texttt{null} for a plain install; and \texttt{command} is exactly what
the FixApplicator runs inside the container. Notebook-code edits
(\texttt{replace\_import}) are deferred to future work -- in practice many
renamed-import cases are still resolved by pinning an older version where
the import was valid, which remains a pip fix.

The example below shows the object's meaningful content, not its literal
persisted field names. The implemented record (\Cref{sec:m-repair}) carries
the same four values -- action, resolved install name, version, and the
evidence behind them -- but names and nests them differently.

\begin{verbatim}
{
  "action": "pin_version",
  "import_name": "scanpy",
  "install_name": "scanpy",
  "version": "1.9.3",
  "command": "python -m pip install scanpy==1.9.3",
  "rationale": "Notebook needs an API present only up to 1.9.x;
                pinning the last compatible release.",
  "pypi_evidence": {
    "latest_version": "1.10.1",
    "chosen_version": "1.9.3",
    "requires_python": ">=3.9"
  }
}
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{The \texttt{repair\_attempts} Table}
\label{subsec:repair-table}

The repair layer writes to a single new SQLite table,
\texttt{repair\_attempts}, which serves a dual purpose: it is the live
validation log (O3) and the reusable benchmark dataset (O4). It records
\emph{one row per repair-decision entry}: an abstention is a row with
\texttt{action = none} and no outcome, a two-round repair produces two
rows, and re-running the same targets under a different model or prompt
produces further rows. Normally an entry corresponds to a RAGRepairAgent
invocation. The one exception is an original record outside repair scope:
it never reaches the agent, and the orchestrator records a synthesised
abstention entry for it instead, so that every original record is
represented (\Cref{sec:m-resultlogger-join}). In the
final evaluation this gives 235 rows per
run (187 Round-1 and 48 Round-2 invocations), of which 73 carry an
executed fix application and its outcome. The error data is never copied
-- each row joins back to the
originating failure via \texttt{notebook\_execution\_id}. Filtering on
\texttt{round}, \texttt{llm\_model}, \texttt{prompt\_strategy}, and
\texttt{run\_id} is what powers the ablation and the model/prompt
comparisons.

\begin{verbatim}
CREATE TABLE repair_attempts (
  id                      INTEGER PRIMARY KEY,
  notebook_execution_id   INTEGER NOT NULL,
                          -- FK -> notebook_executions

  -- from ErrorClassifier
  failing_module          TEXT,          -- e.g. "scanpy"
  subtype                 TEXT,   -- missing_package | wrong_version

  -- from LLMExplainer (O1): this round's explanation
  explanation             TEXT,

  -- from RAGRepairAgent -- the proposed fix (O2)
  action                  TEXT,   -- install | pin_version | none
  install_name            TEXT,          -- resolved PyPI name
  version                 TEXT,   -- version to pin, or NULL
  command                 TEXT,          -- exact command run
  rationale               TEXT,
  pypi_evidence           TEXT,   -- JSON retrieval evidence

  -- from FixApplicator -- the outcome (O3)
  outcome                 TEXT,   -- fixed|still_failing|apply_error
  new_error_type          TEXT,
  new_error_message       TEXT,

  -- run metadata (for experiments)
  llm_model               TEXT,
  prompt_strategy         TEXT,          -- for prompt comparison
  round                   INTEGER,       -- 1 or 2 (hard cap)
  run_id                  TEXT,   -- groups one run / config
  created_at              TEXT,

  FOREIGN KEY (notebook_execution_id)
    REFERENCES notebook_executions(id)
);
\end{verbatim}

\Cref{sec:m-resultlogger} describes how each of the eighteen
non-\texttt{id} columns is populated from the four upstream output files,
including how a \texttt{notebook\_execution\_id} that legitimately repeats
across separate attempts is kept as separate rows rather than collapsed.

\subsection{Knowledge-Graph Enrichment}
\label{subsec:kg}

The FAIR Jupyter knowledge graph~\cite{samuel2024fairkg} is not written by
hand. It is generated from one RML mapping per table, run through
Morph-KGC by the project's existing build script. Objective~O6 adds a
single new mapping for the \texttt{repair\_attempts} table, modelled on the
existing mapping for the executions table. It re-expresses each row as
triples whose notebook IRI matches the notebook resource used by the
existing FAIR Jupyter knowledge graph. When the generated repair RDF is
combined with or loaded alongside the upstream graph, the repair history
can therefore be queried together with the existing reproducibility data.
Following the existing CSV-based RML architecture,
rather than introducing a separate RDF-generation pipeline, keeps the
repair data readable by the same tooling as everything else in the graph.

The vocabulary strategy follows the earlier design notes: \emph{reuse} existing
\texttt{repr:} terms where they already fit (e.g.\ \texttt{repr:exception}),
\emph{add} new \texttt{repr:} terms for repair-specific facts, and use
PROV-O~\cite{provenance2013}
for the activity/agent/time layer, representing each repair as a
\texttt{prov:Activity}. This serialisation of repair provenance directly
serves the Reusable principle of FAIR~\cite{wilkinson2016fair} and the
execution-environment provenance emphasised by
FAIR4RS~\cite{barker2022fair4rs}.
The base IRI is \texttt{repr:}~$=$~\url{https://w3id.org/reproduceme/} and
the serialisation is Turtle (\texttt{.ttl}).

\begin{verbatim}
repr:notebook_512  repr:hadRepairAttempt  repr:repairattempt_001 .

repr:repairattempt_001
    a                       prov:Activity, repr:RepairAttempt ;
    repr:exception          "ModuleNotFoundError" ;  # existing term
    repr:failingModule      "scanpy" ;                # new term

    repr:fixOutcome         "fixed" ;

\section{Demonstration Interface}
\label{sec:demo-interface}

A demonstration interface was built alongside the pipeline to make its
executions and repair results easier to inspect than the raw output files
allow. It is a small, static web application with two views. A dashboard
lists every notebook record of the final evaluation run, with filters for
subtype, final outcome, and Round-2 state. Selecting a record opens a
per-notebook Repair Workspace that steps through the stages this chapter
defines, from the original failure through classification, explanation,
repair, fix application, and re-execution to the final result, including a
second round where one occurred.

This interface is a visualisation layer, not a sixth pipeline component. It
only displays artefacts the pipeline has already produced, and issues no
requests of its own to Ollama, PyPI, GitHub, or Docker. Nothing it does can
influence a repair decision. It played no part in the quantitative
evaluation of \Cref{chap:validation}, and no usability evaluation of the
interface itself is claimed.

\Cref{fig:demo-workspace-failure,fig:demo-workspace-repair,fig:demo-workspace-reexecution}
show three successive stages of the same recorded execution, notebook
\#164, whose Round-1 repair of \texttt{sklearn} succeeds in removing the
targeted error while the notebook as a whole continues to fail on a later,
different dependency error -- the distinction analysed quantitatively in
\Cref{chap:validation}.

Together, these components define the repair-layer architecture:
classification and explanation establish what failed, RAGRepairAgent
proposes a grounded repair, FixApplicator validates it through
re-execution, and ResultLogger preserves the resulting evidence for
evaluation and knowledge-graph integration. \Cref{chap:methodology}
describes how these contracts were implemented, while
\Cref{chap:validation} evaluates the resulting system.
